Project Overview

This illustrated map traces the industrial heritage of the Yangpu riverfront on the north bank of the Huangpu River in Shanghai. It follows a walking route along the southern section of the riverfront, from Qinhuangdao Road to Dinghai Road, and pairs each heritage site with its history, a photo, and a stamp-style icon.

A timeline along the bottom (1860–2007) links the sites into one story: from the birth of modern industry in Shanghai to the conversion of the old factories and warehouses into public space.

Location

Yangpu District lies in northeastern Shanghai. Its riverfront along the Huangpu River was once one of the densest industrial belts in China, lined with waterworks, power plants, textile mills, shipyards, and warehouses.

A Short History

From the late 19th century, the Yangpu riverfront grew into Shanghai's largest industrial zone. Many of China's "firsts" in modern industry were built here, including China's first modern waterworks. Through the 20th century the riverfront was lined with factories, and it became a working waterfront closed off from the city. As industry moved out, the old buildings were preserved and converted. Today the southern section is an open public space, promoted under the idea of the "People's City" (人民城市人民建，人民城市为人民).

  • 1860s The riverfront becomes an industrial area, centered on textile production.
  • 1881–1883 Yangshupu Waterworks is built, and on June 29, 1883, Li Hongzhang opens the sluice. It is China's first modern waterworks.
  • 1884 A German firm founds the Xiangtai Timber Company.
  • 1906 Huangpu Wharf is built. Between 1919 and 1920, students left from here for the Work-Study Movement in France.
  • 1910–1913 Yangshupu Power Plant is planned in 1910 and begins generating power on April 12, 1913.
  • 1920 The Maoma Warehouse is built, on the site of a textile mill founded in 1895.
  • 1921 The No. 17 Cotton Mill is founded. It is now the Shanghai International Fashion Center.
  • 1922 The Yong'an Warehouse is completed. It was once a key storage site for the Wing On Company.
  • 1923 The Shanghai Soap Factory is founded, originally the British-owned China Soap Company.
  • 2007 Construction begins on Fisherman's Wharf, on the site of the former Shanghai Fish Market, China's first fish market.

Heritage Sites Along the Route

  • Huangpu Wharf: departure point of the Work-Study Movement in France
  • Yangshupu Waterworks: China's first modern waterworks, still supplying water today
  • Maoma Warehouse and the Shanghai No. 1 Wool Top Mill
  • Yong'an Warehouse: planned as the World Skills Museum
  • Yangshupu Power Plant Relic Park
  • Shanghai International Fashion Center (former No. 17 Cotton Mill)
  • Shanghai Soap Factory, Fisherman's Wharf, and Fuxing Island
  • Yangpu Riverside People's City Planning Exhibition Hall

The Riverfront Today

The former industrial waterfront has been opened up as a continuous public riverside, and the historic buildings now sit beside new offices, museums, and parks.
