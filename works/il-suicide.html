Spatial Clustering of Suicide Mortality Among Older Adults in Illinois (2015–2023)

Course: GGIS 560 – Spatial Epidemiology
Institution: University of Illinois Urbana–Champaign
Instructor: Dr. Csaba Varga
Project Type: Final Project

Project Overview

Suicide among adults aged 65 years and older is an increasing public health concern in the United States, yet its sub-state spatial patterns remain underexplored.
This project investigates whether older adult suicide mortality in Illinois exhibits spatial clustering and examines how county-level socioeconomic factors are associated with the observed patterns.

Using county-level suicide mortality data and spatial statistical methods, this study integrates exploratory spatial analysis and regression modeling to identify geographic disparities and spatial heterogeneity in risk factors.

Data Sources

  • Illinois Violent Death Reporting System (IVDRS) County-level suicide deaths among adults aged 65+, aggregated for 2020–2023 to mitigate data suppression and small cell counts.
  • American Community Survey (ACS) 5-year estimates Socioeconomic and demographic indicators, including:
    • Population density
    • Older adults living alone
    • Poverty among adults aged 65+
    • Work intensity (≥35 hours/week)
    • Educational attainment
    • Veteran status

Methods

Exploratory Spatial Analysis

  • Choropleth mapping of suicide counts and rates
  • Global Moran’s I to assess overall spatial autocorrelation
  • Incremental spatial autocorrelation to identify optimal spatial distance
  • Getis–Ord Gi* hotspot analysis
  • Anselin Local Moran’s I for cluster and outlier detection

Regression Analysis

  • Exploratory regression and Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)
  • Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR) to assess spatially varying relationships

All spatial analyses were conducted at the county level, with data projected to UTM Zone 16N.

Spatial Distribution of Suicide Mortality

Choropleth mapping reveals substantial geographic variation in suicide mortality among older adults across Illinois.
While higher counts are concentrated in more populous counties, population-adjusted rates highlight elevated risk in several northern and central counties.

Spatial Autocorrelation

Incremental spatial autocorrelation identified a peak clustering distance of approximately 56.8 km, corresponding to the highest z-score.
Global Moran’s I confirmed significant positive spatial autocorrelation (I = 0.2895, p < 0.001), indicating that suicide mortality among older adults is not randomly distributed in space.

Hotspot and Local Cluster Analysis

Hotspot analysis identified statistically significant high-risk clusters in counties adjacent to the Chicago metropolitan area, while cold spots were concentrated in southern and western Illinois.
Local Moran’s I further revealed high–high and low–low clusters, as well as spatial outliers, highlighting localized geographic disparities.

Geographically Weighted Regression Results

A GWR model including population density, living alone, and work intensity was selected based on theoretical relevance and model performance.
Results indicate strong spatial heterogeneity in the relationships between socioeconomic factors and older adult suicide mortality.

Key findings include:

  • Population density is generally negatively associated with suicide mortality
  • Living alone shows a consistently positive association, with stronger effects in southern Illinois
  • Work intensity exhibits substantial regional variation

Key Findings

  • Suicide mortality among older adults in Illinois exhibits significant spatial clustering
  • High-risk clusters are concentrated in northeastern Illinois
  • Socioeconomic determinants show non-stationary spatial relationships
  • Place-based approaches are critical for understanding and addressing late-life suicide risk

Skills Demonstrated

  • Spatial autocorrelation and hotspot analysis
  • GIS-based choropleth mapping
  • Regression modeling (OLS & GWR)
  • Public health and spatial epidemiology analysis
  • Interpretation of spatial heterogeneity in socioeconomic effects

Notes

This project was completed as part of GGIS 560 (Spatial Epidemiology) and emphasizes the use of spatial statistical methods to inform place-based public health interventions.
