Geological Mapping of the Berkeley Hills, California

Course: Field Geology and Digital Mapping
Institution: University of California, Berkeley
Project Type: Final Project
Tools: FieldMove, QGIS, Adobe Illustrator, Adobe Photoshop

Project Overview

This project investigates the geological evolution of the Berkeley Hills, located along the eastern margin of San Francisco Bay, California.
Through field-based geological mapping and digital cartography, the study reconstructs Middle to Late Miocene stratigraphy, volcanism, and structural deformation associated with the transition from a convergent to a transform plate boundary along the San Andreas Fault system.

The project integrates field observations, structural measurements, and GIS-based map production to produce a comprehensive geological map and interpret the tectonic history of the East Bay region.

Study Area

The mapped area covers approximately 10 km² in the Berkeley Hills, including:

  • Grizzly Peak Boulevard
  • Round Top Mountain
  • Sibley Volcanic Regional Preserve

Key mapped units include the Claremont Formation, Orinda Formation, Moraga Formation, Siesta Formation, and Bald Peak Basalt.

Methods

Field Mapping

  • Field data were collected using FieldMove, including:
    • Lithologic descriptions
    • Outcrop locations
    • Strike and dip measurements
    • Georeferenced field photographs
  • Traditional field tools (compass-clinometer, hand lens, rock hammer) were used for validation and sampling.

Digital Mapping & Visualization

  • Field data were imported into QGIS for spatial analysis and map compilation.
  • Final cartographic products were refined using Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop.
  • Base maps include Google Terrain imagery and contour data (NAD83, UTM Zone 10N).

Geological Map

The final geological map synthesizes stratigraphy, faults, folds, and volcanic units across the study area.

Stratigraphy & Depositional Environments

Claremont Formation (Middle–Late Miocene)

  • Deep-marine chert, shale, and siliceous sediments
  • Deposited in a subsiding forearc basin
  • Represents bathyal marine conditions prior to regional uplift

Orinda Formation (Late Miocene)

  • Fluvial sandstones, conglomerates, and red siltstones
  • Records uplift, erosion, and terrestrial sedimentation
  • Indicates basin reorganization linked to fault activity

Moraga Formation (Late Miocene)

  • Basaltic lava flows, tuffs, lapillistone, and volcaniclastic breccia
  • Reflects slab-gap volcanism associated with Mendocino Triple Junction migration

Volcanism in the Sibley Area

The Sibley Volcanic Regional Preserve preserves a well-exposed sequence of Moraga Formation volcanics, including lava flows, tuffs, and vent-related deposits.

These units document alternating explosive and effusive eruptions, interbedded with sedimentary intervals during volcanic quiescence.

Structural Interpretation

Structural analysis reveals significant deformation related to regional compression and strike-slip faulting:

  • Siesta Valley Syncline: NE–SW trending fold
  • Hayward Fault Zone: Active right-lateral strike-slip fault system

Key Outcomes

  • Produced a detailed digital geological map integrating field and GIS data
  • Reconstructed Miocene tectonic, volcanic, and sedimentary evolution
  • Demonstrated the use of digital mapping workflows in field geology

Skills Demonstrated

  • Field geology and stratigraphic interpretation
  • Structural measurements and stereonet analysis
  • GIS-based geological mapping
  • Scientific visualization and cartographic design

Notes

This project was completed as part of EPS 101 at UC Berkeley and represents an end-to-end workflow from field data collection to digital geological interpretation.
